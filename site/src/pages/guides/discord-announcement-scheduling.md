Discord servers run on announcements: patch notes, event reminders, stream alerts, the weekly community call. Most of them are written ahead of time and posted by hand at the right moment, which means someone has to be there at that moment. Scheduling takes that job away.

**Discord is coming soon to PostDelegate. It cannot be connected today.** This page explains where it stands, what to expect, and which channels you can schedule community announcements to right now.

## What to expect

On Discord, scheduled posts normally come from a bot rather than from your own account. Expect the same here: you add PostDelegate to a server you manage, then choose which channel each post goes to, much as you pick a channel for Slack today. The exact flow, settings and limits will be documented when the connection opens, and may differ from what other tools do.

- **Twitch and Kick chat.** Schedule stream reminders and highlighted Twitch announcements for people who are watching. See [Twitch and Kick chat announcements](/guides/twitch-and-kick-chat-announcements).

One post can go to several of these channels at once, with the text adjusted per channel, so a release note or event reminder can reach every community from one place.

## Get your announcement routine ready

A few things carry over to any channel, including Discord when it opens:

1. **Decide who approves.** Announcements to a whole community are hard to take back. In **Settings > Approvals**, turn on **Require approval before publishing** so posts from members who can't approve wait for review. See the [approval workflow guide](/guides/client-approval-workflow).
2. **Keep a standard format.** Save a reusable starting point under **Settings > Sets**, and a standard sign-off under **Settings > Signatures**. Both are available on paid plans.
3. **Label your announcements.** Use [campaigns and tags](/docs/campaigns) so release notes, events and reminders are easy to find on the calendar.

## A good structure for community announcements

Whatever the channel, announcements read better when they follow one shape:

1. **One line that says what happened.** "Version 2.3 is out."
2. **What people need to do.** "Update before Friday's event."
3. **Where the details are.** A link, or a follow-up with the full notes.
4. **Who to ask.** A channel or a person.

Ping everyone only for things that affect everyone. A community that is notified about every small update learns to mute the channel.
